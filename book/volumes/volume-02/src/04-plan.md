# How This Volume Is Arranged

The volume follows the order in which a robot is actually built: the body first, then the muscles, then the nerves, then the behaviour, and finally the workshop and the world.

**Part One** asks what a robot is, and what a body has to do. It covers structure, stiffness, joints, degrees of freedom and workspace. By the end you will be able to count the freedom of a mechanism and predict what it cannot reach.

**Part Two** is about power: motors, gears, batteries, wiring and heat. These chapters contain the arithmetic that decides whether a design is possible, and they are the ones to read with a pencil.

**Part Three** is about nerve: drivers, electronics, sensors and signal quality. It is the part most often skipped and most often responsible for a machine that works on the bench and not on the floor.

**Part Four** is about behaviour. It starts with doing exactly what you are told, which is harder than it sounds, and moves to simple feedback that survives small disturbances. It ends with the chapter on safety, which is not an appendix to the subject but a condition of doing it at all.

**Part Five** is about making: a first robot of your own, and a tour of the machines that already work for a living.

Each chapter ends with something to do. Some of the exercises need only paper; a few need a phone, a ruler or a cheap motor. Where a real component is required, a substitute is offered, because nobody should be stopped by a shopping list.
