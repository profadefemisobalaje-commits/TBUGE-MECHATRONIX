# How This Volume Is Arranged

**Part One — Language as Data** takes the machine's view. Text arrives as characters, is cut into tokens by rules that are choices rather than discoveries, and turns out to be wildly uneven: a handful of words account for most of the tokens and most of the vocabulary accounts for almost nothing. Counting is the first skill, and the chapters here do it on small, checkable examples.

**Part Two — Meaning Without Understanding** builds the classical statistical apparatus: words as vectors derived from the company they keep, sequences as chains of small probabilities, and the arithmetic of the unseen. The last of these is the technical heart of the volume, because every language model that has ever been built is, at bottom, a strategy for assigning a small probability to something it has never seen.

**Part Three — Learning From Text** moves to models trained on data: the context window, text classification, extraction, and retrieval. This is where the discipline of splitting and evaluating becomes decisive, since a text dataset can be contaminated in ways a numerical one cannot.

**Part Four — Language in the World** is about the applications that people actually buy: translation, speech interfaces, and the decision to put a language system in front of a user. The closing chapter is a checklist and an argument, and it is the one to read twice.

The chapters vary in shape on purpose. One is built around a table of counts, one around a worked translation, one around a failure in a call centre. A uniform template would make the book easier to write and worse to read.

Every chapter ends with work, under a heading that suits the work: Problems, Exercises, Working Notes, Things to Try.
