# How This Volume Is Arranged

**Part One** establishes the vocabulary: what a learning problem is, where data comes from, and what it means to be wrong. It contains no algorithms, and it is the part to reread when a project starts behaving strangely.

**Part Two** builds four methods in order of increasing flexibility: a straight line, a probability, a neighbourhood, and a tree. Each chapter fits the method on a small table of numbers, examines what it can and cannot express, and names the way it fails.

**Part Three** is the discipline. It covers overfitting as a trade between two kinds of error, validation as a procedure that can be done correctly or incorrectly, and evaluation as a decision about costs rather than a single number.

**Part Four** takes the model out of the notebook. It covers the pipeline that turns raw columns into predictions, the deployment that keeps a model alive, and the cases where the honest answer is that machine learning is the wrong tool.

Every chapter ends with work: calculations to do by hand, small experiments on data you can invent, and one question that has no single right answer. The calculations matter more than the reading, because the failure modes of this subject are numerical and only practice makes them visible.
